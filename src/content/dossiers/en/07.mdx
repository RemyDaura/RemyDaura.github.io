<Section label="Context" title="Three hierarchical levels, three needs">

Sanitoral runs projects across several countries and regions, with three hierarchical levels whose needs are very different: the Executive Board, the Regional Directors and the Country Directors.

</Section>

<Section label="Need" title="One question per reader">

Turn raw project tracking data (costs, deadlines, deliverables) into a single decision support system, able to answer three different questions depending on who is looking:

<Callout title="Three questions, one tool">

1. Are we meeting our overall objectives?
2. Which region is the weak link?
3. Which specific project is drifting, and why?

</Callout>

</Section>

<Section label="Approach" title="A funnel, from overview to detail">

The scoping relied on a Product Strategy Canvas, setting out three critical user stories, one per hierarchical level. This funnel structure, from overview to detail, shaped the whole architecture of the report.

Data preparation was designed to last: in Power Query, an automated cleaning pipeline removes stray headers and types currencies and dates correctly. The benefit for Sanitoral is direct: each new monthly file goes into the source folder, and one click on "Refresh" is enough, with no manual rework.

The data model cascades around a central pivot table, `Projects_plans`. Upstream, one-to-many relationships with the Geography and Project Type axes let a Director filter by country or type. Downstream, one-to-one relationships keep actual data (costs, duration) separate from forecasts, so what is planned and what is observed are never mixed.

The result is cascading navigation: a "Helicopter" view for the Executive Board, with immediate indicators (budget spent, projects at risk), from which three clicks lead to the corrective action on a specific project.

<Section label="Results" title="IT drifts on deadlines, Marketing on costs">

<Stats items={frontmatter.stats} />

The analysis revealed a clear strategic pattern: IT projects systematically drift on deadlines, while Marketing projects tend to drift on costs. The resulting recommendation comes with figures: increase the Marketing forecast budget by 10% and extend IT schedules by 15%, to set more realistic targets and reduce the number of false alerts cluttering the steering.

</Section>

<Section label="Limits and next steps" title="To be confirmed over several cycles">

- The automated pipeline assumes a stable file structure from one month to the next; a change of format at the source would require adapting the Power Query steps.
- The drift thresholds (+20% on IT deadlines, Marketing cost gaps) should be tracked over several more cycles to confirm that they are structural rather than one-off.

</Section>
