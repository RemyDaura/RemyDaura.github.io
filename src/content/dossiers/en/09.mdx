<Section label="Context" title="A complete health check">

Lapage's Marketing Department wants a complete review of the online bookshop's financial health, and a detailed reading of its customers' behaviour, in order to draw concrete levers for action.

</Section>

<Section label="Need" title="Levers, not just charts">

Go beyond charts: identify strategic levers that can genuinely be acted on to increase revenue, from a dataset that has been made reliable.

</Section>

<Section label="Approach" title="The right test for each question">

Three sources were combined (products, customers, transactions), explicitly excluding spurious transactions from internal IT tests, to guarantee an entirely real dataset: about 680,000 sales analysed.

On overall revenue (about €12 million), a 30-day moving average smoothed out daily and weekly variations to reveal a stable underlying trend.

The most significant finding came from a Lorenz curve and a Gini coefficient of 0.44: just four customers account for 7.3% of total revenue, nearly €1 million. They are clearly business customers (libraries, purchasing groups), set aside from the rest of the analysis so they would not distort the reading of individual customers' behaviour.

For individual customers, a Shapiro-Wilk test showed that purchase amounts do not follow a normal distribution, which ruled out Pearson's test in favour of a Spearman correlation between age and purchase frequency (r = 0.21, weak but significant).

A paradox then appeared: older customers buy often but with small baskets, younger customers buy rarely but with very large baskets. A Levene test ruled out a classic ANOVA in favour of a Kruskal-Wallis test (p = 0.0) and an eta squared of 0.111, confirming that the paradox is explained by the category of books bought: younger customers focus on a category that is much more expensive than the one older customers prefer.

</Section>

<Section label="Results" title="Three levers for three audiences">

<Stats items={frontmatter.stats} />

Three recommendations followed:

- a VIP programme with a dedicated account manager, to secure the €1 million generated by the 4 B2B customers;
- targeted social media campaigns around the book category popular with younger customers, to increase how often they visit;
- bundled offers ("packs") to increase the basket size of older customers, who are already loyal.

</Section>

<Section label="Limits and next steps" title="What the data could not show">

- Margin data was not available: it was impossible to check whether the best-selling products are also the most profitable.
- No geographic data was available to compare the behaviour of customers in Paris and in the rest of France.
- The age/frequency correlation, although statistically significant, remains weak (0.21): other factors need exploring to refine the profile of individual customers.

</Section>
